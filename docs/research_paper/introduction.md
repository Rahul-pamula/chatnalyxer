# I. INTRODUCTION

## A. Background
The paradigm of digital communication has undergone a profound transformation, characterized by a decisive shift away from asynchronous mediums like email toward synchronous instant messaging platforms. Applications like WhatsApp and Telegram have emerged as primary conduits for interpersonal communication globally. Initially designed for social interaction, these platforms are increasingly co-opted for academic and professional coordination. In educational institutions, students and faculty frequently establish WhatsApp groups for courses and project collaborations. These groups serve as centralized hubs for rapidly disseminating critical information, including assignment deadlines, examination schedules, and meeting links. Furthermore, communication within these platforms has evolved from simple text exchanges to complex, multi-modal interactions. Modern chat streams are populated with heterogeneous data, including photographs of whiteboards, voice notes detailing project requirements, and PDF files containing assignment briefs. Consequently, instant messaging platforms have become informal repositories of organizational knowledge. Despite this critical role, these applications inherently lack the structural framework required for effective task tracking, operating entirely on a chronological interface that prioritizes the most recent interaction over the most important information.

## B. Problem Statement
The heavy reliance on instant messaging for organizational coordination has precipitated a pervasive and escalating issue: conversational information overload. In active group chats, particularly those comprising dozens or hundreds of participants, the volume of messages generated daily can be staggering. Critical temporal information—such as a specific deadline for a software engineering project or a last-minute change to a lecture venue—is frequently communicated in a single, informal message. Within a matter of hours, this crucial data point can be buried beneath hundreds of subsequent messages consisting of casual conversations, acknowledgments, or unrelated queries. Because the interface of these applications is inherently linear and lacks sophisticated semantic search or automatic categorization capabilities, users are forced to continuously monitor the chat or manually scroll through extensive message histories to locate important details. This phenomenon, often referred to as the "scroll and miss" effect, consistently results in overlooked deadlines, missed meetings, and forgotten assignments. The problem is exacerbated by the multi-modal nature of the data; a deadline communicated via a rapid voice note or embedded within an image of a handwritten schedule is effectively invisible to standard text-based search functions within the application. Consequently, the burden of data extraction, synthesis, and organization falls entirely on the user, creating an environment where critical tasks are easily lost within the conversational noise.

## C. Research Gap
While the problem of information overload in digital communication is well-documented, existing technological solutions fail to adequately address the specific challenges posed by modern instant messaging platforms. Conventional task management and calendar applications, such as Jira, Trello, and Google Calendar, offer robust organizational frameworks but necessitate active, manual data entry. The user must recognize a task within a chat, switch applications, and manually transcribe the date, time, and context—a process that introduces significant friction, disrupts cognitive flow, and is highly susceptible to human error or procrastination. Conversely, automated Information Extraction (IE) systems have historically focused on formal, structured, or semi-structured text, such as news articles, legal documents, and corporate emails. Traditional Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques, including standard Named Entity Recognition (NER) models, struggle significantly with the colloquial, fragmented, and highly contextual nature of instant messaging. Phrases like "next Tuesday at five" or "tomorrow morning" require dynamic temporal resolution relative to the timestamp of the message, a capability often absent in conventional parsers. Furthermore, existing extraction tools are predominantly unimodal, focusing exclusively on text. They lack the architectural capacity to seamlessly ingest and process the heterogeneous media—images, voice notes, and PDFs—that frequently contain the most critical scheduling data in contemporary group chats. There is, therefore, a substantial research gap in the development of passive, multi-modal extraction systems capable of autonomously structuring informal conversational data without requiring explicit user intervention.

## D. Motivation
The primary motivation for this research is to bridge the widening gap between the informal mediums through which tasks are communicated and the structured systems required to manage them effectively. The cognitive load associated with actively filtering, remembering, and manually cataloging tasks from a continuous stream of casual messages is a significant source of stress and inefficiency for students and professionals. By automating this process, technology can restore the utility of instant messaging platforms as effective coordination tools while mitigating their chaotic side effects. Furthermore, the rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) and cloud-based AI services presents an unprecedented opportunity to solve this problem. LLMs now possess the zero-shot reasoning capabilities required to parse ambiguous, informal language and accurately resolve temporal context. Simultaneously, cloud-based Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and Speech-to-Text services have achieved high levels of accuracy. The motivation of this work is to synthesize these cutting-edge AI technologies into a cohesive, privacy-preserving application that operates invisibly in the background, transforming unstructured chaos into actionable, structured productivity, thereby preventing academic and professional penalties associated with missed deadlines.

## E. Proposed Solution
To address the challenges of conversational information overload and the limitations of existing manual and unimodal extraction tools, this paper proposes Chatnalyxer, an automated, multi-modal system designed to passively monitor targeted WhatsApp group chats and autonomously extract actionable tasks and temporal events. Chatnalyxer is engineered as a robust, distributed architecture comprising a secure message ingestion service, an advanced AI processing pipeline, a centralized backend API, and an intuitive mobile client. The system integrates directly with the WhatsApp network utilizing the Baileys library via a Node.js microservice, establishing a secure, authenticated WebSocket connection via standard QR code linking. This allows the system to passively receive messages in real-time from user-whitelisted groups, ensuring privacy by ignoring unauthorized chats. Upon receiving a message, the payload is securely transmitted to a FastAPI-based backend. Here, the system executes a sophisticated multi-modal preprocessing pipeline depending on the media type. Text messages are routed directly to the extraction engine. Images are processed using Azure AI Optical Character Recognition (OCR) to extract embedded text. PDF documents are analyzed utilizing Azure Document Intelligence, and voice notes are transcribed using Azure Cognitive Speech. Regardless of the original modality, the resulting text is subsequently injected into a highly optimized prompt alongside the message's precise UTC timestamp. This composite prompt is processed by Google Gemini AI, a Large Language Model capable of advanced semantic understanding. The LLM is tasked with determining if the message constitutes a task, assignment, or event, and if so, resolving casual temporal expressions into standardized ISO-8601 datetime formats. The extracted data, including the task name, precise deadline, and inferred priority level, is persistently stored in a PostgreSQL database. Finally, the system employs APScheduler to manage background cron jobs, orchestrating smart, priority-based push notifications to a React Native mobile application, alerting users only when necessary.

## F. Major Contributions
This research presents several significant contributions to the fields of Applied Artificial Intelligence, Information Extraction, and Human-Computer Interaction. The major contributions of this paper are enumerated as follows:
1) Development of a Multi-Modal Extraction Pipeline: We propose and implement a novel architecture capable of ingesting and unifying heterogeneous data payloads from instant messaging streams. By seamlessly integrating OCR, document parsing, and speech-to-text transcription with an LLM-based extraction engine, the system ensures that critical temporal data is captured regardless of whether it is communicated via text, image, document, or audio.
2) Context-Aware Temporal Resolution of Casual Language: We introduce an effective prompt engineering methodology that injects precise chronological metadata (UTC timestamps) into the LLM context window. This approach successfully overcomes the limitations of traditional NER systems by enabling the LLM to accurately resolve ambiguous, relative, and colloquial temporal expressions typical of informal chat environments.
3) Design of a Privacy-Preserving, Ephemeral Processing Architecture: Recognizing the sensitive nature of personal communications, we contribute an architectural design pattern prioritizing data minimization. The system is designed such that raw message payloads and media files are processed ephemerally in memory and immediately discarded post-extraction, with only the structured task metadata being persistently stored in the database.
4) Implementation of an End-to-End Passive Monitoring System: We provide a comprehensive, functional implementation of the Chatnalyxer system, demonstrating the practical viability of bridging unstructured communication platforms with structured, automated calendar and notification systems without requiring active user data entry.
