The proliferation of instant messaging platforms for academic and professional coordination has resulted in significant information overload, frequently causing users to miss critical deadlines buried within high-volume conversational noise. While dedicated task management systems exist, they necessitate active, manual data entry, disrupting workflow and increasing cognitive load. Furthermore, conventional information extraction techniques struggle to accurately parse temporal events from the informal, fragmented, and multi-modal nature of group chats. To address this research gap, this paper introduces Chatnalyxer, an automated, multi-modal system designed to passively monitor targeted communication channels and autonomously extract actionable tasks. The proposed architecture leverages Large Language Models, specifically Google Gemini AI, to perform context-aware temporal resolution of casual expressions. To accommodate heterogeneous data payloads, the system integrates Azure AI Services for Optical Character Recognition, document parsing, and voice transcription, enabling comprehensive extraction across text, images, PDFs, and audio messages. The methodology involves secure WebSocket-based ingestion of messaging data, asynchronous multi-modal preprocessing, and context-injected LLM prompting that resolves relative temporal references against precise UTC timestamps. A key contribution of this work is the development of an ephemeral data processing pipeline that ensures user privacy by discarding raw media immediately post-extraction. Preliminary performance evaluations indicate that the multi-modal LLM approach achieves high precision and recall in identifying implicit and explicit deadlines within conversational text, while operating within acceptable latency bounds for background execution. Consequently, the system significantly reduces the necessity for manual calendar management. In conclusion, Chatnalyxer presents a scalable, privacy-preserving framework for transforming unstructured, multi-modal chat data into structured organizational events, effectively mitigating the consequences of conversational information overload.
